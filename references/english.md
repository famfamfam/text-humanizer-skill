# English editing notes

Use these notes for English passages. Judge the sentence by its meaning and
audience, not by whether it contains a word associated with AI writing.

## Wording and register

- Prefer an ordinary verb to an inflated verb phrase when nothing changes:
  "make a determination" can become "determine". Retain specialist terminology
  when a simpler substitute would lose precision.
- Preserve tense, aspect, and uncertainty. "Has been testing" does not mean
  "has tested"; "could reduce" does not mean "reduces". Hedges can carry evidence
  limits rather than indecision.
- Use contractions if they fit the established voice. Do not force "we're" or
  "you'll" into formal notices, or remove contractions from a casual email
  merely to sound more professional.
- Preserve the source's variety of English unless a target is specified.
  Do not casually switch spelling, date order, punctuation conventions, or
  region-specific vocabulary. Ask about a numerical date only if resolving it
  is necessary to make the requested edit.
- Active voice is useful when the actor is known and relevant. Keep passive
  voice when the actor is unknown or the object deserves emphasis.

## Structure and emphasis

Place a request where the reader can find it; preserve any supplied reason and
deadline. Keep an argument's necessary evidence even if the result is less
punchy. For spoken copy, check awkward clause nesting and breath length.
For instructions, keep dependencies and warnings alongside the relevant action.

Remove an introduction or concluding sentence when it contributes no information.
Keep signposting such as "however" when it expresses a real contrast. Do not ban
em dashes, lists, rhetorical questions, or any particular word as evidence of AI.
Avoid adding a motivational ending, sales pitch, or personal aside unasked.

## Examples with stable meaning

| Source | Possible edit | What stays intact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We would like to request that you send the signed form by 18 September. | Please send the signed form by 18 September. | The document and deadline. |
| This setting could lower memory use when processing large files. | This setting could lower memory use when processing large files. | The uncertainty and condition; no edit is needed. |
| A decision was made to postpone the meeting until Thursday. | The decision was to postpone the meeting until Thursday. | No invented decision-maker or claim that the move is already complete. |
| According to the sales team, demand may increase by up to 8%. | The sales team estimates that demand may rise by up to 8%. | Attribution, uncertainty, and the upper bound. |
| The service does not store uploaded files after processing is complete. | The service does not keep uploaded files after processing. | Negation and when retention ends. |

These are examples of editorial decisions, not substitution templates. Their
dates and numbers are not facts to reuse in the user's text.
